# Project Procurement Management

---

## 1. Introduction to Project Procurement

Procurement is an essential part of project management. It involves acquiring goods, services, or software from external suppliers.

**Contracting** is a core business activity. Contracts vary significantly in:
- Value
- Duration
- Complexity

### Acquiring Software from External Suppliers
Software can be acquired in three main ways:
- **Bespoke (Custom-built)**: Developed specifically for the customer.
- **Off-the-shelf (COTS)**: Bought as-is.
- **Customised Off-the-Shelf (COTS)**: Standard software modified to meet specific needs.

## 2. Goods vs Services vs Licenses

| Type       | Description                                      | Example                          | Ownership                     |
|------------|--------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Goods**  | Physical products                                | Laptop, car                      | Transferred to buyer          |
| **Services** | Work performed by people                       | Software development, maintenance| N/A                           |
| **Licenses** | Permission to use software                    | Microsoft Office, Adobe          | Supplier retains ownership    |

**Note:** Most software is purchased via **licenses** rather than full ownership.

## 3. Types of Contracts

### 3.1 Fixed Price Contracts
- Price is **fixed** at the time the contract is signed.
- Customer requirements must be **very well defined**.
- Changes require contract renegotiation.

**Advantages:**
- Customer knows exact cost upfront.
- Supplier is motivated to control costs.

**Disadvantages:**
- Supplier may inflate price to cover risks.
- Changes are expensive.
- Risk to product quality (supplier may cut corners).

---

### 3.2 Time and Materials Contracts
- Charged at a fixed hourly/daily rate.
- Supplier invoices regularly for actual work done.

**Advantages:**
- Flexible — easy to change requirements.
- Less pressure on quality (supplier not forced to cut corners).

**Disadvantages:**
- Customer bears most of the risk.
- No strong incentive for supplier to be efficient.

---

### 3.3 Fixed Price per Unit Delivered
- Supplier quotes a fixed price **per unit** (e.g., per Function Point).
- Final price = Unit price × Number of units.

**Example (RDI Technologies):**
- 2,000 FPs × $967
- 500 FPs × $1,019
- 100 FPs × $1,058
- **Total = $2,549,300**

**Advantages for Customer:**
- Transparent pricing.
- Easy to compare suppliers.
- Supplier still motivated to be efficient.

**Disadvantages:**
- Accurate software sizing (Function Points) is difficult.
- Handling changes to existing functionality is complex.

## 4. Other Contract Classification

Based on selection approach:
- **Open Tendering**: Any supplier can bid.
- **Restricted Tendering**: Only invited suppliers can bid.
- **Negotiated Procedure**: Direct negotiation with one or few suppliers (e.g., extensions).

---

## 5. The Tendering Process

### Key Stages:
1. **Requirements Analysis** (Most critical step)
2. **Evaluation Plan** development
3. **Invitation to Tender (ITT)** / Request for Proposal (RFP)
4. **Evaluation of Proposals**
5. **Contract Award**

**Note:** Poor or incomplete requirements are the **main cause** of procurement disputes.

---

## 6. Requirements

### Functional Requirements
Describe **what** the system must do.
- Examples:
  - The system shall authenticate users.
  - The system shall generate daily patient lists.

### Non-Functional Requirements
Describe **how** the system performs (quality attributes).
- Often poorly written and hard to test.
- Must be made **measurable**.

**Types of Non-Functional Requirements:**
- Product requirements (performance, availability)
- Organizational requirements (security policies)
- External requirements (legal, regulatory)

**Good Example (Measurable):**
> "Experienced users shall complete all functions after 2 hours training, with no more than 2 errors per day."

---

## 7. Evaluation Plan
Methods to evaluate proposals:
- Reading proposals
- Interviews
- Demonstrations
- Site visits
- Practical tests

**Value for Money (VFM)** assessment is essential.

---

## 8. Contract Checklist

Important sections to include:
- **Definitions** (clear terminology)
- **Form of Agreement** (sale, lease, license?)
- **Goods/Services to be supplied** (detailed specifications)
- **Timetable** and milestones
- **Payment arrangements** (often milestone-based)
- **Ownership of Software** (copyright, source code, escrow)
- **Progress reporting**
- **Change management** (how variations are handled)
- **Acceptance criteria**

---

## 9. Contract Management

Key ongoing activities:
- Progress monitoring and reporting
- Managing variations/changes
- Milestone reviews and payments
- Acceptance testing
- Dispute resolution

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Clear and complete requirements** are the foundation of successful procurement.
- Choose the **right contract type** based on project uncertainty and risk allocation.
- **Fixed Price** = Low risk for customer, high risk for supplier.
- **Time & Materials** = High flexibility, high risk for customer.
- **Procurement success** depends on thorough evaluation and strong contract management.
