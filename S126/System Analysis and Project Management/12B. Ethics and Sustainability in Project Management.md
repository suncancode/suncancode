## 1. Introduction to Ethics

### What is Ethics?
Ethics refers to the moral principles that govern a person's behavior or the conducting of an activity. It involves distinguishing between right and wrong and making decisions that align with moral values.

### What is Business Ethics?
Business ethics is the application of ethical principles and moral values to business practices and decision-making. It guides how organizations and professionals should behave in the workplace, especially when facing dilemmas involving profit, people, and societal impact.

### Why is Being Ethical Important in Project Management?
Being ethical is critical for project success and professional credibility. Key benefits include:

- Elevates the profession and raises future standards.
- Increases the faith and trust others place in project managers.
- Imprints positive moral mindsets and behaviors on individuals.
- Improves business relationships at all levels.
- Promotes fair decision-making.
- Reduces project risks.
- Provides a greater chance of project success.
- Reduces anxiety, stress, and turnover within project teams.

**Source:** TechRepublic – “The Importance of Being Ethical in Project Management”

## 2. Project Management Institute (PMI) Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct

The PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct outlines the expectations for project management practitioners worldwide. It emphasizes doing what is **right and honorable**.

The Code is divided into **four core sections**:

### 2.1 Responsibility
**Definition:** Take ownership for the decisions we make (or fail to make), the actions we take (or fail to take), and the consequences that result.

#### Aspirational Standards (What we *should* do):
- Make decisions and take actions based on the best interests of society, public safety, and the environment.
- Accept only assignments consistent with our background, experience, skills, and qualifications.
- Fulfill the commitments we make — do what we say we will do.
- Take ownership and correct errors or omissions promptly.
- Report errors or omissions caused by others to the appropriate body.
- Protect proprietary or confidential information.

#### Mandatory Standards (What we *must* do):
- **Regulations and Legal Requirements:** Inform ourselves about and uphold all relevant policies, rules, regulations, and laws. Report unethical or illegal conduct.
- **Ethics Complaints:** Bring violations of the Code to the appropriate body. Only file complaints when substantiated by facts. Do not retaliate against those raising ethical concerns.

---

### 2.2 Respect
**Definition:** Show a high regard for ourselves, others, and the resources entrusted to us (people, money, reputation, safety, and environmental resources).

#### Aspirational Standards:
- Inform ourselves about the norms and customs of others and avoid disrespectful behavior.
- Listen to others’ points of view and seek to understand them.
- Approach directly those with whom we have conflict.
- Conduct ourselves professionally, even when not reciprocated.

#### Mandatory Standards:
- Negotiate in good faith.
- Do not use expertise or position to influence others for personal benefit.
- Do not act abusively toward others.
- Respect the property rights of others.

---

### 2.3 Fairness
**Definition:** Make decisions and act impartially and objectively. Conduct must be free from competing self-interest, prejudice, or favoritism.

#### Aspirational Standards:
- Demonstrate transparency in decision-making.
- Continuously re-examine our impartiality and take corrective action.
- Provide equal access to information for authorized stakeholders.
- Make opportunities equally available to qualified candidates.

#### Mandatory Standards:
- Proactively disclose any real or potential conflicts of interest.
- Refrain from decision-making or influencing outcomes until conflicts are disclosed, mitigated, and approved by stakeholders.
- Do not hire, fire, reward, punish, or award contracts based on favoritism, nepotism, or bribery.
- Do not discriminate based on gender, race, age, religion, disability, nationality, or sexual orientation.
- Apply organizational rules without favoritism or prejudice.

---

### 2.4 Honesty
**Definition:** Understand the truth and act truthfully in communications and conduct.

#### Aspirational Standards:
- Earnestly seek to understand the truth.
- Be truthful in communications and conduct.
- Provide accurate information in a timely manner.
- Make commitments and promises in good faith.
- Create an environment where others feel safe to tell the truth.

#### Mandatory Standards:
- Do not engage in or condone deceptive behavior (misleading statements, half-truths, withholding information).
- Do not engage in dishonest behavior for personal gain or at the expense of others.

## 3. ACS Code of Ethics (Australian Computer Society)

The ACS Code of Ethics provides ethical guidelines specifically relevant to IT professionals in Australia.

### Core Values:
- **The Primacy of the Public Interest**  
  Place the interests of the public above personal, business, or sectional interests.

- **The Enhancement of Quality of Life**  
  Strive to enhance the quality of life of those affected by your work.

- **Honesty**  
  Be honest in your representation of skills, knowledge, services, and products.

- **Competence**  
  Work competently and diligently for your stakeholders.

- **Professional Development**  
  Enhance your own professional development and that of your colleagues and staff.

- **Professionalism**  
  Enhance the integrity of the Society and the respect among its members.

## 4. Main Ethical Concerns in Managing IT/Software Projects

Common ethical challenges in IT projects include:
- Data privacy and security
- Intellectual property protection
- Conflicts of interest
- Transparency in reporting project status and risks
- Fair treatment of team members and stakeholders
- Balancing business pressures with public interest
- Responsible use of emerging technologies (AI, automation, etc.)
- Environmental impact of technology solutions

## 5. Sustainability in Project Management

### What is Sustainability?
Sustainability is **“the ability to exist constantly.”**  
The most widely accepted definition of **sustainable development** is:

> “Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.”  
> — World Commission on Environment and Development (1987)

### Key Aspects of Sustainability in Project Management

Sustainability involves balancing **three pillars**:
- **Social**
- **Environmental**
- **Economic**

Additional characteristics:
- Balances short-term and long-term orientation
- Considers both local and global impacts
- Is grounded in values and ethics
- Emphasizes transparency and accountability
- Requires stakeholder participation
- Focuses on risk reduction and eliminating waste
- Consumes income rather than capital

### Sustainable Project Management Definition

> **“Sustainable Project Management is the planning, monitoring and controlling of project delivery and support processes, with consideration of the environmental, economic and social aspects of the life cycle of the project’s resources, processes, deliverables and effects, aimed at realizing benefits for stakeholders, and performed in a transparent, fair, and ethical way that includes proactive stakeholder participation.”**

**Authors:** A.J. Gilbert Silvius and Ron P.J. Schipper (2014)

## Summary

Ethical conduct and sustainability are not optional add-ons in modern project management — they are **core competencies**. Following the PMI and ACS codes helps project managers build trust, reduce risks, and deliver projects that create genuine long-term value for society and the environment.

**Key Takeaway:**  
Great project managers don’t just deliver projects on time and within budget — they do so **responsibly, respectfully, fairly, honestly**, and with consideration for **future generations**.
